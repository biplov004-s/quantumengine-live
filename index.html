Live quantum optimization cycle

A quantum circuit picks the portfolio, not a hunch.

This engine runs a real QAOA (Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm) circuit on IBM's Qiskit simulator to solve the stock-selection problem — genuine quantum computation, applied to a rotating batch of the Nifty 500 universe each cycle.

Classical scoring narrows 500 names down to a shortlist. The shortlist is handed to a quantum optimizer, which searches the combinatorial space of possible portfolios simultaneously — something classical brute-force struggles to scale.

How a signal actually gets made

Three stages, two different kinds of computing. This is the honest pipeline — no step is skipped or faked.

01

Shortlist the field

Momentum and volatility scoring ranks the full stock universe. The top names move forward — quantum hardware today can't hold hundreds of decision variables at once, so classical computing does the wide scan.

classical
02

Optimize with QAOA

The shortlist becomes a QUBO — maximize expected return, minimize covariance risk, pick exactly k names. A parameterized quantum circuit searches that space and is measured to find the best bitstring.

quantum circuit
03

Publish the signal

Selected names are marked BUY, the rest AVOID for this cycle. The result is checked against the exact classical optimum, so you can see whether the quantum solver actually found the best answer.
